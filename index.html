What's in scope — and what isn't

South Africa's Legal Practice Act reserves certain functions for admitted, practising attorneys and advocates. As a non-practising attorney working as a consultant, here's exactly where the line sits.

✅ What I do

  • Contract and policy drafting
  • Legal research memos and written opinions
  • POPIA / PAIA compliance advisory
  • NGO governance documents
  • Immigration and citizenship application support
  • Plain-language legal content for organisations

🚫 What I refer out

  • Court representation and litigation
  • Conveyancing and notarial work
  • Criminal defence
  • Anything requiring an admitted attorney's right of appearance
Why this is lawful: The Gauteng High Court confirmed in Damons N.O. and Another v Bezuidenhout (25 August 2025) that non-admitted legal consultants may lawfully draft documents, research, and give opinions for a fee. The Legal Practice Act reserves specific functions only — court appearance, conveyancing, notarial work — not advisory work generally.
